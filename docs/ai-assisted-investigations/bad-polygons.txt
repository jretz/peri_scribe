Goal and context
The input is raw WFIGS/IRWIN daily fire perimeters — geometry produced by rapid mapping (aircraft/satellite) at scale, and it is messy in ways that desktop GIS tolerates but Google Earth's polygon renderer does not. GE fills polygons by tessellation, and two classes of source artifacts break that tessellation: degenerate rings (fill silently dropped) and dense near-coincident segments (fill comes out in bands). The cleaning's job is to remove below-resolution artifacts while preserving everything that is real fire boundary. Two functions cooperate: clean_perimeter() (every perimeter that goes into the KML) and clean_growth() (the growth rings computed by differencing two perimeters).

The four artifact classes (measured, not theoretical)
- Zero-area sliver parts — Crosswhite has 89 of its 98 polygon parts with area 0.0 (Little Giant 187, Babylon 135). They render as invisible zero-area outlines: pure noise.
- Zero-area interior rings ("holes") — Crosswhite's main polygon carries 14 degenerate hole rings (up to 1,288 points folded onto a line); Kaiser has 29, Babylon 19. A degenerate innerBoundaryIs makes GE drop the entire polygon's fill — the first "outline only" symptom.
- Hairline slits — the perimeter path doubles back within meters of itself: Crosswhite's ring has 40,145 segment pairs closer than 10 m. GE's tessellator can't resolve these, producing the vertical filled/unfilled bands.
- Difference slivers — g2.difference(g1) between two independently-mapped perimeters generates thousands of sub-acre boundary slivers (Crosswhite's 3,700-ac growth arrived as 15,806 polygons).

`clean_perimeter()` — the shared pipeline, stage by stage
1. Area floor for parts — reject any part with area ≤ 1e-6 sq deg (~2 ac at these latitudes). Zero-area slivers and sub-resolution specks disappear before any heavier work.
2. Douglas–Peucker ring simplification — simplify the exterior ring at simplify_tol=0.0002° (~22 m) with preserve_topology=True. This is the slit killer: segments closer than the tolerance get merged, collapsing the doubled-back paths (Crosswhite: 40,145 → 7 near-pairs, 46,407 → 5,470 points) while keeping 95% of the perimeter path. The preserve_topology flag guarantees simplification can't introduce self-intersections.
3. Hole filtering + simplification — interior rings are kept only if their area exceeds the same floor, then simplified the same way. A zero-area hole is, by definition, not a real unburned island.
4. Validity backstop — rebuild the polygon from the cleaned rings; if it's still invalid (buffer(0)), which returns the valid topological area and dissolves any remaining near-touching slivers.
5. Reassembly — parts are re-collected into a MultiPolygon (or a single polygon), so the KML writer never sees the source's junk.

`clean_growth()` — the differencing pre-pass
Growth geometry is derived, so it gets its own hygiene before the shared pipeline:
1. Project to EPSG:5070 (Albers equal-area) — area-based filtering is only meaningful in a metric CRS; degrees distort area by latitude.
2. Area floor of 5 ac — the difference slivers are sub-acre; anything under 5 ac in projected units is boundary noise, not new fire.
3. Simplify at 0.0001° (~11 m) — the difference operation produces jagged edges; this smooths them.
4. `union_all()` dissolve — merges the kept parts into one clean geometry.
5. Back to 4269, then `clean_perimeter()` — the same ring/hole/validity stages as the base perimeters, so growth rings can't reintroduce degenerate rings either.

Why the parameters are what they are
- 1e-6 sq deg ≈ 2 ac at these latitudes — small enough to keep real islands, large enough to reject zero-area artifacts (the degenerate rings measure exactly 0.0, so the floor could be far smaller; the margin is safety).
- 0.0002° ≈ 22 m — the smallest tolerance that collapses the measured slit population (10 m pairs → ~0 at 22 m; measured: 40,145 → 7). Larger tolerances (0.001°, 110 m) also work but start eating genuine detail (84% path), so 22 m is the minimum effective dose.
- 5 ac for growth — difference slivers are sub-acre by construction; genuine new area for these fires is hundreds-to-thousands of acres, so there's an enormous margin.
- Measurement CRS (5070) ≠ output CRS (4269) — areas are computed in an equal-area projection; coordinates are written in NAD83/WGS84 as KML requires. The two are deliberately separated.

Design principles
- Measure in the right CRS, write in the output CRS. Never filter on degree-area; never write projected coordinates to KML.
- Order of operations matters: reject by area before simplifying (cheap filters first), simplify before validity-fixing (validity repair is the backstop, not the norm).
- `preserve_topology=True` everywhere — cleaning must not create the very self-intersections it's preventing.
- Thresholds are minimum-effective-dose, not aesthetic choices — each was set by measuring the artifact population and picking the smallest setting that eliminates it.
- Fidelity bias — nothing real is removed: genuine narrow inlets stay (the 7 remaining near-pairs are actual fire boundary), the two-snapshot growth narrative is untouched, and the cleaned geometry is what the acreage labels now report.

One honest footnote: the pipeline is tuned to this dataset's artifact spectrum (WFIGS/IRWIN daily perimeters). The stages are general — degenerate-ring rejection, topology-preserving simplification, validity repair — but the specific thresholds were measured from these 10 fires. If you feed it different source data, the right move is to re-measure the artifact population first rather than assume the constants transfer.
